LLM Basics — 6 topics

  1. Tokens & tokenization

    Models do not see characters or words — they see tokens, sub-word chunks from a fixed vocabulary. Everything you pay for and every limit you hit is counted in tokens, and roughly 1 token ≈ 4 characters of English (much worse for code and other languages).

  2. Embeddings & vector space

    An embedding maps text to a vector where distance means semantic similarity. This is the mechanism behind search, clustering and RAG — and the reason "dog" and "puppy" land near each other while "dog" and "dogma" do not.

  3. How a transformer generates text

    The model runs your tokens through attention layers, produces a score for every token in its vocabulary, and one gets sampled. Then it appends that token and does the whole thing again — which is why output is slow and linear in length while input is processed in parallel.

  4. Context window & its limits

    The context window is everything the model can see at once — system prompt, history, retrieved chunks and the answer being generated all compete for it. Attention cost grows quadratically with length, and models genuinely attend less well to the middle of a long context.

    Resources

    Practice

  5. Sampling — temperature, top-p

    The model outputs a probability distribution; sampling decides what to do with it. Temperature 0 is near-deterministic and right for extraction and classification; higher values trade reliability for variety. Most production bugs blamed on "the model" are really sampling settings.

  6. Training, fine-tuning & when not to

    Pretraining, instruction tuning and RLHF explain why a chat model behaves as it does. Practically: fine-tuning teaches form, retrieval supplies facts — so most problems people reach for fine-tuning to solve are actually RAG or prompting problems.
