Threading — 5 topics

  1. Threads & thread pools

    Starting a thread per task stops scaling quickly — pools bound the count and reuse workers. ThreadPoolExecutor gives you submit, map and futures, and is almost always what you should reach for over raw Thread objects.

  2. Race conditions & atomicity

    Two threads reading, modifying and writing the same value will lose updates — x += 1 is three operations, not one. A race is a bug that appears under load and vanishes when you add a print statement, which is exactly why it must be prevented by design.

  3. Locks, deadlock & starvation

    Mutexes, RLocks, semaphores and conditions — and the three ways they fail. Deadlock needs four conditions to hold simultaneously, and the practical fix is almost always consistent lock ordering. Hold locks for as short a time as you can.

  4. Thread-safe communication

    The better answer to shared state is not sharing it — pass messages instead. queue.Queue is thread-safe and turns coordination into producer and consumer, which is dramatically easier to reason about than a web of locks.

  5. Thread-local state & immutability

    Two ways to avoid locking entirely: give each thread its own copy with threading.local(), or never mutate shared data in the first place. Immutable data is automatically thread-safe — the cheapest correctness you can buy.
