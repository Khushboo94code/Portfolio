Data in Production — 4 topics

  1. Managed vs self-hosted databases

    A managed Postgres handles backups, patching and failover for real money; self-hosting is cheaper and every one of those becomes your job. For anything with users, take the managed option until you have a specific reason not to.

  2. Connections & pooling

    Every Postgres connection costs memory, and the limit is lower than people expect. Django opens one per worker, so workers × instances can exhaust it fast — CONN_MAX_AGE helps, PgBouncer is the real fix.

  3. Backups & restore

    A backup you have never restored is a hope, not a backup. Know your RPO (how much data you can lose) and RTO (how long you can be down), automate pg_dump or snapshots, store them off the server, and rehearse the restore.

  4. Running migrations safely

    Migrations run against live data while old code may still be serving requests. The safe pattern is expand → migrate → contract: add the new column, backfill it, switch the code, then drop the old one — never all at once.
