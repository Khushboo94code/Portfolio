Algorithms — 6 topics

  1. Recursion & backtracking

    Explore a choice, recurse, then undo the choice. Every subset, permutation and combination question is the same skeleton with a different constraint — and pruning invalid branches early is what separates a passing solution from a timeout.

  2. Greedy

    Take the locally best option and never reconsider. Fast and short when it works — and wrong when it doesn't, so the real skill is justifying why the greedy choice is safe. Usually pairs with sorting by the right key.

  3. Dynamic programming — 1D

    Overlapping subproblems solved once and reused. Start from the recursive brute force, add memoisation, then convert to a bottom-up table. Defining the state — what dp[i] actually means — is the whole problem; the transition usually follows.

  4. Dynamic programming — 2D & knapsack

    Two-dimensional state, typically comparing two sequences or tracking a capacity alongside an index. Grid paths, edit distance, longest common subsequence and the knapsack family are the templates worth knowing cold — most 2D DP questions are a variation on one of them.

  5. Bit manipulation

    XOR's self-cancelling property, masks, and n & (n-1) to clear the lowest set bit. Niche but high-yield — several problems have a two-line bitwise answer and no other solution under the constraints. Bitmasks also encode subsets for DP.
