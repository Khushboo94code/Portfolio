Foundations — 5 topics

The layers and addressing scheme everything else assumes. Worth getting solid before touching the protocol details.

  1. OSI & TCP/IP models

    The layering that lets a protocol at one level ignore the one below it. Seven OSI layers in theory, four TCP/IP layers in practice — what matters is being able to place any technology on the stack and say what it does and doesn't guarantee.

  3. Switching, routing & NAT

    How a packet finds its way: switches forwarding by MAC within a network, routers forwarding by IP between them, and NAT letting many private addresses share one public one. NAT is why your laptop can reach a server but the server can't reach back.

    Resources
    Learn
    • Routing — Wikipedia
    • NAT — Wikipedia
    • Network Protocols — the routing chapters
    Practice
    • Run traceroute to a server abroad and explain every hop that responds.
    • Find your private and public IP and explain why they differ.

  4. DNS

    Turning a name into an address: recursive versus authoritative resolvers, the record types (A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, TXT), and TTL-driven caching at every level. DNS caching and propagation delay are behind a large share of "it works on my machine" incidents.

  5. DHCP & ARP

    The two protocols that quietly make a local network work: DHCP hands out addresses when a device joins, ARP maps an IP to the MAC address on the wire. Mostly invisible until an ARP cache goes stale or a lease expires at the wrong moment.
