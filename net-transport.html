Transport Layer — 4 topics

TCP and UDP are where most interview questions actually land — and where most real performance problems live.

  1. TCP — handshake & reliability

    The three-way handshake, sequence and acknowledgement numbers, retransmission, and the four-way close with TIME_WAIT. Reliability isn't free: every guarantee TCP gives you costs a round trip, which is why connection reuse matters so much.

    Resources

    • High Performance Browser Networking — Building Blocks of TCP
    • Hussein Nasser (YouTube) — TCP internals series
    • Beej's Guide — sockets, the API view of TCP
    Practice
    • Capture a handshake in Wireshark and identify SYN, SYN-ACK and ACK.
    • Explain what TIME_WAIT is for and why a busy server accumulates them.

  2. Flow & congestion control

    Two different problems people conflate: flow control stops a fast sender overwhelming a slow receiver (the window), congestion control stops it overwhelming the network (slow start, AIMD). Bandwidth-delay product explains why a fat, distant link can still feel slow.

    Resources

    • TCP congestion control — Wikipedia
    • Network Protocols
    Practice
    • Compute the bandwidth-delay product for a 100 Mbps link at 150 ms RTT, and say what window size it needs.

  3. UDP & when to use it

    No handshake, no ordering, no retransmission — and that's the point. The right choice when late data is worse than lost data: DNS, video calls, games, and QUIC, which rebuilds reliability on top of UDP in user space to escape TCP's constraints.

  4. Ports, sockets & connection state

    A connection is identified by the four-tuple of source IP, source port, destination IP and destination port — which is why one server port can hold thousands of connections. Knowing the socket states explains ephemeral port exhaustion and "address already in use".
